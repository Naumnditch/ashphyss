What the 0625 exam actually wants 0625

An object moves in a circle at constant speed because a force acts on it perpendicular to its motion — towards the centre.

Speed stays the same but direction keeps changing → velocity is changing → it is accelerating.

no force towards the centre → no circle. It just goes straight on (Newton 1).

Describe it qualitatively (mass m, speed v, radius r):

  • Bigger force, same m & r → faster
  • Bigger force, same m & v → smaller radius (tighter circle)
  • Bigger mass, same v & r → needs a bigger force

↳ all three come from F = mv²/r (box ③, ★ ext)

1Angles & going round

θ = s / rrad ★ ext

1 turn = 2π rad = 360°  ·  1 rad ≈ 57.3°

Period T = time for one full circle (s)
Frequency f = turns per second (Hz) → f = 1/T

ω = Δθ/Δt = 2π/T = 2πfrad/s ★ ext

ω = angular speed: angle swept per second. Every point on a spinning wheel has the same ω, but the rim has the biggest v:

v = ωr v = 2πr / T0625

v = 2πr/T is just distance ÷ time (circumference ÷ period). It's in 0625 for orbits!

Try it: A fan spins at 600 revolutions per minute. The blade tip is 0.30 m from the axle. Find f, T, ω and the tip speed.

show solutionhide solution
  1. f = 600 ÷ 60 = 10 Hz → T = 1/f = 0.10 s
  2. ω = 2πf = 2π × 10 = 62.8 rad/s
  3. v = ωr = 62.8 × 0.30 = 18.8 m/s ≈ 19 m/s
  4. check: v = 2πr/T = (2π × 0.30) ÷ 0.10 = 18.8 m/s ✓

2Which way is a?

  • velocity: along the tangent, direction always changing
  • acceleration: always towards the centre = centripetal ("centre-seeking")
  • a ⟂ v → it changes the direction only, never the speed
a = v²/r = ω²rm/s² ★ ext

Same speed, half the radius → double the acceleration. Tight bends are harder!

Try it: A car goes round a bend of radius 50 m at a steady 15 m/s. Find its centripetal acceleration and its angular speed.

show solutionhide solution
  1. a = v²/r = 15² ÷ 50 = 225 ÷ 50 = 4.5 m/s² towards the centre of the bend
  2. ω = v/r = 15 ÷ 50 = 0.30 rad/s
  3. check: ω²r = 0.30² × 50 = 4.5 m/s² ✓ (the car's mass isn't needed)

3Centripetal force ★ ext

Newton 2: resultant F = ma, and a = v²/r, so the resultant force needed is

F = mv²/r = mω²rN

It points towards the centre, ⟂ to the velocity, so it does no work: speed (and KE) stay constant.

"Centripetal" is a JOB, not a new force! Some real force (or forces) does the job ↓

situationwhat pulls it to the centre?
ball whirled on a stringtension in the string
car on a flat bendfriction (tyres on road)
Moon, planets, satellitesgravity
electron round a nucleuselectric attraction
car on a banked trackhorizontal part of the normal force

Double the speed → 4× the force (v²). Halve the radius → 2× the force.

Try it: A 0.75 kg mass on a string moves in a horizontal circle of radius 1.1 m, making 12 revolutions every minute. Find the tension in the string.

show solutionhide solution
  1. period T = 60 ÷ 12 = 5.0 s
  2. ω = 2π/T = 2π ÷ 5.0 = 1.26 rad/s
  3. tension provides the centripetal force: F = mω²r = 0.75 × 1.26² × 1.1
  4. F ≈ 1.3 N (towards the centre)

4Real-world circles ★ ext

Method every time: ① draw the real forces → ② find the resultant towards the centre → ③ set it equal to mv²/r.

Vertical circle (ball on a string)

Just make it over the top when T = 0: vmin = √(gr)

Horizontal circles: roads

  • Flat: friction = mv²/r. Too fast, or icy road → not enough friction → car skids outwards along a tangent.
  • Banked: N cosθ = mg and N sinθ = mv²/r → tanθ = v²/(rg)
    at this "design speed" no friction is needed at all.

Try it: A bend of radius 80 m is designed for 20 m/s with no friction. At what angle should it be banked? (g = 9.8 m/s²)

show solutionhide solution
  1. tanθ = v²/(rg) = 20² ÷ (80 × 9.8) = 400 ÷ 784 = 0.510
  2. θ = tan⁻¹(0.510) = 27°

Worked example: the conical pendulum

Resolve the tension T:

  • vertical (no up/down motion): T cosθ = mg
  • horizontal (to the centre): T sinθ = mv²/r
  • divide: tanθ = v²/(rg) and radius r = L sinθ

Try it: A 0.20 kg bob on a 0.50 m string swings in a horizontal circle with the string at 30° to the vertical. Find the radius, the tension, the speed and the time for one revolution. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

show solutionhide solution
  1. radius r = L sinθ = 0.50 × sin 30° = 0.25 m (not 0.50 m!)
  2. tension T = mg / cosθ = (0.20 × 9.8) ÷ cos 30° = 2.3 N
  3. v² = rg tanθ = 0.25 × 9.8 × tan 30° = 1.41 → v = 1.2 m/s
  4. time for one turn = 2πr / v = (2π × 0.25) ÷ 1.19 = 1.3 s

5Energy in circles

  • Uniform (horizontal) circle: speed constant → KE = ½mv² constant. The centripetal force is ⟂ to the motion, so it does zero work.
  • Vertical circle: GPE changes with height, so the speed changes. Top: GPE max, KE min. Bottom: KE max.
    ½mvbottom² = ½mvtop² + mg(2r) ★ ext
  • Orbits 0625: circular orbit → speed and height constant → KE and GPE both constant. Elliptical orbit → closer to the Sun it goes faster (GPE → KE); further away it slows (KE → GPE). Total energy stays constant.

Try it: A 0.10 kg ball on a 0.80 m string is swung in a vertical circle, only just keeping the string taut at the top. Find its speed at the top, its speed at the bottom and the tension at the bottom. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

show solutionhide solution
  1. top, T = 0: mg = mv²/r → vtop = √(gr) = √(9.8 × 0.80) = 2.8 m/s
  2. energy, top → bottom (falls 2r = 1.6 m):
    vbottom² = vtop² + 2g(2r) = 7.84 + 31.36 = 39.2
  3. vbottom = 6.3 m/s
  4. bottom: T − mg = mv²/r → T = 0.98 + (0.10 × 39.2 ÷ 0.80) = 0.98 + 4.9 = 5.9 N — six times the weight!

Energy gives the SPEED. F = mv²/r then gives the FORCE. Use both!

7Traps & tips

  • "There's an outward centrifugal force." No outward force acts. You feel thrown out because your body tries to go straight on (inertia) while the car turns.
  • Adding "Fc" as an extra arrow on a force diagram. Draw only real forces (tension, weight, friction, normal). Their resultant is the centripetal force.
  • "Constant speed, so no acceleration." Velocity is a vector. Changing direction = accelerating, towards the centre.
  • "If the string breaks it flies straight outwards." It leaves along the tangent, in the direction it was moving at that instant.
  • F = mv/r or F = mv² (forgetting a bit). F = m × v²/r: it's still F = ma, with a = v²/r. Check units: kg × m²/s² ÷ m = kg m/s² = N.
  • Using degrees in ω, or the calculator in the wrong mode. ω is in rad/s. rpm → ω: ÷ 60 then × 2π. Use DEG mode for sin 30°, RAD mode if the angle is in radians.
  • Using the diameter, or the string length L, as r. r is the radius of the circle the object travels. Conical pendulum: r = L sinθ.
  • Mixing up the two T's. T = period (s) in v = 2πr/T, but T = tension (N) on force diagrams. Write "period" or "tension" in words when both appear.
